### Grouping Notes
- Separated what the frame *is made of* (Genuine Solid Wood, Reclaimed Wood, Mango Wood, Wood & Glass Materials) from how the wood *looks* (Natural Grain & Weathered Texture, Bark & Live Edges, Color & Finish), and gave bark/live edges their own group because it is a distinct design feature, not just texture.
- Tags that pair a material with a strength claim ("durable solid wood frame," "sturdy solid wood construction," "sturdy and well-made wood") → Well-Made & Sturdy Build, because the stated benefit is sturdiness; plain material tags ("solid wood frame") stayed in Genuine Solid Wood.
- Split glass tags four ways by benefit: what it is (Real Glass Front), how it looks (Clear, Vivid Viewing), how tough it is (Tempered, Scratch-Resistant Glass), and what it does for photos (Glass Protects Photos); "tempered high-clarity glass" → Tempered group since tempering is the more specific attribute. Plexiglass and polished safety edges kept separate.
- Display options split into orientation, wall-or-tabletop placement, tabletop stand, included easel + hooks, wall hardware only, and ease of hanging. The two tags naming both orientation and placement got their own small group rather than being forced into either; "vertical and horizontal hanging display" → Orientation since placement (wall) is only implied.
- Backing tags split into easy insertion (tabs, removable back), secure hold (fasteners, levers, turn buttons), and backing board quality; "easy photo insertion and changes with turn button" → Easy Photo Insertion because ease is the stated benefit.
- Shape tags split into Wavy Border (a distinct, repeated style) vs. general Frame Shape & Proportions vs. Dimensional Details & Profiles (depth, layering, ridges); "classic detail" and "nice detail" → Dimensional Details as the closest fit, though both are vague.
- Vague tags placed by best guess: "good feel" and "peel-resistant edges" → Well-Made & Sturdy Build (quality/durability of the object); "practical and versatile photo frame" → Photo & Artwork Fit; "beautiful wood frame" → Beautiful Color & Finish (general appearance praise); "varied mango wood grain pattern" → Natural Grain rather than Mango Wood, because the benefit is the grain look.
- "solid wood frame with bark edge, glass pane, and mdf backboard" is a full materials list → Wood & Glass Materials; "hand-carved mango wood frame" → Handcrafted, since hand-carving is the more specific claim. Several single-tag groups (odorless wood, deer backdrop, matting, sizes, gift packaging) had no close match and were left on their own.

### Grouping Notes
- Grouped by what went wrong for the customer rather than by part or spec: size complaints with different numbers (24x36, 5x7, 6x9) share one group because the root problem is the opening not matching what was expected.
- Consequences follow their root cause: glare, reflectiveness, milkiness, and scratching/discoloration all went with Plastic Instead of Real Glass, since each traces back to the non-glass cover.
- Kept weak hardware (hooks, tabs, mounts that fail) separate from awkward setup (drilling, off-center hooks, time to set up), per the "part is weak" vs. "task is awkward" rule.
- "mat opening hides business card information" → Opening Not the Expected Size, because the underlying issue is a mat opening too small for the item, not a style or display problem.
- "no way to attach a hanger for flush wall mounting" → Awkward Hanging Setup rather than Missing Parts, since the complaint is about the design not allowing a hanger, not a part left out of the box.
- "matting not included" → Missing Parts, but it may reflect a mismatch with the listing rather than a packing error; it could move to a "not as advertised" group if more tags like it appear. "corner split" and "mold on backing board on arrival" → Arrived Damaged, though a split corner could also be a manufacturing defect.
- Left "little metal tabs need to be pressed down" and "peg stand is difficult to use" as separate single-tag groups because they are different tasks (changing photos vs. standing the frame up); they could merge into a broader "hard to use" group if needed.
- "indents damage photos" is vague about what the indents are (likely backing clips or tabs), so it stayed on its own.
