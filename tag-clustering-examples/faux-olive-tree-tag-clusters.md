### Grouping Notes
- Separated how the tree *can be adjusted* (Bendable, Shapeable Branches) from how full it *looks* (Full, Dense Foliage), even though many shaping tags mention fullness; tags that name an action (adjust, fluff, shape, spread) went to shaping, tags that describe the result went to fullness.
- Split the realism theme by part of the tree (overall look, leaves, olives, trunk and bark, branches, branch seams), because shoppers judge each part separately and a single "realistic" group would be too vague. Tags naming several parts at once ("realistic branches, leaves, and olives," "realistic olive tree appearance with branches and fruit") went to Realistic Overall Look; "realistic bark and leaves" and "realistic trunk and leaves" went to Trunk & Bark because the trunk is named first and is the more distinctive detail.
- Split the pot into three meanings: what's included (Pot, Basket & All Parts Included), how it looks (Attractive Pot & Woven Basket), and a plain nursery pot meant to be hidden in another container. Weight and tip-resistance tags about the pot ("heavy plastic vase," "sturdy pot and basket provide stability") went to Heavy, Stable Base because their stated benefit is stability.
- Split size into four groups by the customer's use: general room fit, corner filling, compact/space-saving, and tall/large. "suitable width for a bedroom corner" went to Corner-Filling Size rather than Right Size for the Room because the corner is the stated purpose.
- Split assembly into three groups: general ease and speed, guidance aids (numbers, stickers, instructions), and construction that reduces steps (pre-assembled trunk, three pieces, twist-lock). "numbered branches and internal metal rods for assembly" went to Numbered Parts rather than Wire-Reinforced Branches because its benefit is guided assembly. "easy to assemble and shape" went to Quick, Easy Assembly because assembly is named first.
- Borderline calls: "sways in the breeze" → Realistic Overall Look (natural movement); "fade-resistant olive fruits" and "prevents wilting leaves and fallen branches" → Long-Lasting, Fade-Resistant Color (benefit is longevity, not fruit realism or shedding); "no watering or dying" → No Watering or Sunlight Needed; "suitable for low-light spaces" → Indoor, Outdoor & Low-Light Use (placement) rather than the no-sunlight care group; "pollen-free, bug-free, and odor-free" → Safe, Allergy-Free Materials rather than No Plastic Smell; "great shape" → Full, Dense Foliage; "thick pole" → Realistic Trunk & Bark; "excellent model" → Durable, Quality Build as a general quality comment.
- Input issues: "branches require manual arrangement," "regular dusting required," and "requires regular dusting" read as caveats or care instructions rather than benefits; "regular dusting" got its own group, and "branches require manual arrangement" was kept with shaping. "lightweight" conflicts with the many heavy-base tags (likely a different listing) and stands alone. "off-white color" probably describes the pot, not the foliage, but was placed in Pleasing Color since it doesn't say. "vase" alone is vague and was read as an included container.

### Grouping Notes
- Grouped by what went wrong for the customer: shipping damage, not as advertised (height, fullness), poor realism, weak or loose parts, awkward setup, and pot/basket issues. All "arrives" or "in shipping" tags went to Arrived Damaged or Bent, including "basket arrives smashed in shipping and loses its shape" and "warped bucket does not sit flat," since the root problem is condition on arrival rather than a weak part.
- Kept the weak part apart from the awkward task: Stiff, Hard-to-Bend Branches (the branches themselves resist) vs. Needs Lots of Fluffing (the branches work but shaping takes effort). Likewise, Flimsy Basket (weak part) stays separate from Small or Unattractive Basket (looks and size).
- Consequences went with their root cause: "loose sections cause tree to lean" → Sections Don't Connect Securely rather than Tips Over & Unstable, because the lean comes from the joints, not the weight balance.
- Borderline calls: "smaller and thinner than pictured" → Shorter Than Advertised (it names a size mismatch against the listing) rather than Sparse, Thin Foliage; "thin build" → Sparse, Thin Foliage (read as a thin-looking tree, though it could mean flimsy construction); "too few olives for a realistic look" → Fake-Looking Leaves & Trunk (stated problem is realism); "base is too small" → Pot Too Small (read "base" as the pot, though it could be a stability complaint); "missing leaves expose plastic branch pieces" → Visible Joints & Plastic Parts (the complaint is what shows) rather than Leaves & Branches Fall Off.
- "assembly stickers leave residue behind" was grouped with the other sticker and label problems; it could be split out if residue becomes a recurring theme.
- Kept Fake-Looking Base Filler separate from the foliage realism group because it concerns the pot surface (fake dirt, painted top), which shoppers can cover or replace independently of the tree.
- Kept Choking Hazard as its own group rather than merging it with detaching parts, because safety complaints warrant separate visibility. It also contrasts with the feature tag "securely attached olives," suggesting variation across listings.
- Kept Pot Not Included, Plain Pot Needs Replacing, and Hard to Re-Pot separate: each describes a different root problem (missing part, disappointing look, poor fit), even though all push customers toward buying another container.
