### Grouping Notes
- Separated what the wood *is* (Natural Wood Construction) from how it *looks* (Natural Grain & Warm Color), how it was *made* (Handmade Craftsmanship), how the surface *feels* (Smooth Finish), and what the coating *does* (Food-Safe, Waterproof Coating).
- Split size tags by what the size is good for: serving a group (Large Capacity), everyday and family salads (Right Size), and fitting the space (Counter-Friendly). Generic size praise with no scale ("nice size," "perfect size") goes to the everyday group rather than the large group.
- Size tags that name a volume benchmark ("suitable size for costco caesar salad," "suitable for holding a week's worth of fruit") go to Large Capacity, because they describe how much the bowl holds rather than what it is used for.
- Place mixed tags by their main subject: "beautiful and sturdy acacia wood" → Natural Wood Construction (the species is the subject), not Durability or Color; "handmade premium wood construction" and "timeless handcrafted design" → Handmade Craftsmanship, not Wood Construction or Design.
- Place surface tags by the benefit they state: "smooth wood surface rinses easily and resists odors and stains" → Easy Cleaning, not Smooth Finish; "smooth waterproof surface" → Food-Safe, Waterproof Coating, because waterproofing comes from the coating.
- "good quality" is vague; placed in Durability as general build-quality praise. It could stand alone if more general quality tags appear.
- "suitable for daily use and special events" (occasions) was merged into Versatile Serving Uses rather than kept as a single-tag group; split it out if more occasion tags appear.
- Input issue: the list names both acacia and mango wood, and size tags range from "family of four" to "8-12 people," so the tags likely come from several different listings or size options. Check which applies before using material or capacity claims in product copy.

### Grouping Notes
- Separated problems by when they happened: damage present on arrival ("serving spoon arrives split") is a different root problem from wear that appears after long use ("bowl edges crack at the seam..."), even though both involve splitting wood.
- "serving spoon arrives split" → Arrived Damaged rather than a poor-quality-utensil group, because the tag says the damage was present on arrival.
- "bowl edges crack at the seam..." → a long-term durability group; the "1.5 years" detail was left out of the description as a spec, since the root problem is cracking with extended use.
- "smaller than expected" is vague: it doesn't say whether the listing was wrong or the buyer misjudged the size. It sits alone as a size problem; if future tags name an advertised size, rename the group to "Not the Advertised Size."
- Input issue: "smaller than expected" contrasts with the many large-capacity feature tags, and a "seam" implies a joined bowl rather than one carved piece. Both support the earlier note that these tags may span several listings or sizes.
